Reweighting is a change of environment

Weight each (label, attribute) group by

w = (1−α) + α·n / (4·n_g)

The weighted data is exactly a new environment:

p_α = p − α·(p − ½)

Best choice for a known test env q: α* = (p−q)/(p−½), which can exceed 1.

p = 0.95: group balancing (α = 1) gets 0.84 when reversed; α* = 1.89 gets 0.926 (theory 0.928).

6

This is our main contribution. The standard fix is to reweight the four groups defined by label and attribute so they count equally. We interpolate between normal training at alpha zero and full balancing at alpha one. The key observation: the total weight stays one, so the reweighted data is exactly another environment, with correlation p minus alpha times p minus one half. It is linear in alpha. That gives the whole trade-off curve on the right in closed form, and the dots are experiments landing on it. It also tells us the best alpha for a known test environment, and that it can be bigger than one: if you expect the shortcut to reverse, you should over-balance. At p equals 0.95 that reaches 92.6 percent, versus 84 for standard balancing.

A controlled setting

Two features: one stable, one shortcut

Xs = Y + noise(1.0)

Stable: same in every environment, but noisy

Xz = A + noise(0.2)

Shortcut: A = Y with probability p, else −Y

Train at p = 0.6, 0.8, 0.95. Test at p, 0.5 and 0.1 (reversed). 10 seeds, n = 500 to 8000.

To study this exactly, we built a small synthetic version. Each example has two features. Xs is the stable one: it always relates to the label the same way, but it is noisy. Xz is the shortcut: almost noiseless, but its relation to the label depends on p, which changes between environments. On the left plot, training data: the shortcut separates the classes almost perfectly. On the right, the reversed test environment: the black boundary learned in training now sends most points to the wrong side. Because we wrote down the distribution, we can compute the optimal classifier and its accuracy exactly.

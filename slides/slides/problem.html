The problem

A model learns the corner marker, not the digit

In training, a dark corner square means “1” 95% of the time. The digit itself is right only 75% of the time (we flipped 25% of labels).

A CNN trained normally follows the square on 100% of test images. When the square flips, accuracy drops to 10%.

This is shortcut learning. Can we predict how much it hurts, and how much a fix helps?

Here is the problem in one picture. These are real MNIST zeros and ones. We painted a small square in the top-left corner: dark usually means one, light usually means zero. In training the square matches the label 95 percent of the time, while the digit only matches 75 percent, because we flipped a quarter of the labels on purpose. A normal CNN takes the easy route: it reads the square, not the digit. When we flip the square at test time, it gets only 10 percent right. That is shortcut learning. Our question: can we predict this failure, and predict how well the standard fixes work?

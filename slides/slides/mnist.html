Does this carry over to images and neural networks? Back to MNIST. Because the CNN reads the digit almost perfectly, the problem reduces to two yes-or-no cues: the digit, right 75 percent of the time, and the marker, right with probability p. The model should follow whichever is more reliable in the reweighted training data. Using the same p-alpha formula, we predicted it switches from the marker to the digit at alpha 0.44, and to the inverted marker at 1.56. The dashed lines are the prediction, the dots are the CNN averaged over 3 seeds. Far from the switch points the match is close; near them the change is gradual rather than a sharp jump, which we report honestly as a limitation.
